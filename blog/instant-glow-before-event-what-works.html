Instant Glow Before an Event: What Actually Works in 24–72 Hours

By Dr. Punit Sakhare, MD Dermatologist · 2026-11-02 · 8 min read

Quick Facts

What "glow" really is
Smooth, well-hydrated skin that reflects light evenly — mostly a surface effect you can improve in days, not new collagen, which takes months.
Best 24–72 hour option
A gentle exfoliating treatment like a HydraFacial or a very superficial peel — removes dead-cell buildup and hydrates, with little or no downtime.
Leave enough lead time
Anything that can cause flaking, redness or a purge (medium peels, first-time retinoids, aggressive extractions) needs 2–4 weeks before the event, not the day before.
What's mostly hype
"Glow drips" and IV glutathione for an instant event glow — current evidence does not support intravenous glutathione for skin lightening and flags safety concerns.
The safe rule
Never try a brand-new treatment for the first time right before an important day — test it well ahead so you know how your skin reacts.

"Can you make my skin glow for Saturday?" is one of the most common requests we hear before a wedding, a reception, a shoot or a big festive night. The honest answer is: yes, to a point — and the key is knowing what genuinely changes in a day or two versus what needs weeks. A real, lasting glow comes from healthy, hydrated skin with an even surface and good collagen, and collagen takes months to build. But the look of glow — smooth texture, even light reflection, a plumped, hydrated finish — can be meaningfully improved in 24–72 hours with the right gentle treatment.

The mistake that ruins more event-day skin than anything else is trying something strong or new at the last minute: a deep peel three days before a wedding, a first-ever retinoid the week of a shoot, an aggressive "clean-up" facial that leaves the skin red and bumpy. This guide separates what actually works in the final days from what needs to be booked earlier, and from what the evidence says simply doesn't deliver — so you can spend your time and money on the things that show up on camera.

What "Glow" Actually Is (and Why Timing Matters)

When people say skin looks "glowing," they're usually describing three physical things working together: a smooth surface that scatters light evenly rather than looking rough or flat, good hydration that plumps fine lines and gives a dewy finish, and even tone without dull patches or visible congestion. None of those three require growing new collagen overnight — which is fortunate, because you can't. Collagen remodelling from treatments like microneedling, radiofrequency or lasers happens gradually over roughly 6–12 weeks, as fibroblasts lay down and reorganise new tissue.

What you can change quickly is the top layer. Through each day, dead skin cells accumulate on the surface (the stratum corneum). When that layer is thick and uneven — which happens faster with sun exposure, pollution, and in Mumbai's humidity, a film of sweat, sunscreen and grime — skin looks dull because light hits the rough surface and scatters messily instead of reflecting cleanly. Gently clearing that buildup and deeply hydrating the skin is what creates an "instant" glow.

The catch is that the same exfoliation, pushed too hard or done too late, causes flaking, stinging and redness. A deeper peel literally works by injuring the upper skin so it sheds and renews — brilliant on a four-week timeline, a disaster three days out. So the entire game before an event is matching the intensity of what you do to the time you have left. Get that match right and skin looks its best on the day; get it wrong and you spend the event hiding flaking patches under makeup.

The Best 24–72 Hour Treatment: Gentle Exfoliation + Hydration

For the final two to three days, the treatment that reliably improves glow with minimal risk is a gentle, hydrating exfoliation — most commonly a HydraFacial (hydradermabrasion). It combines a mild exfoliating/peel step, gentle vortex suction to clear pores of dead cells, sebum and debris, and simultaneous infusion of hydrating serums such as hyaluronic acid and antioxidants, with essentially no downtime. That no-downtime profile is exactly why it suits an event: you walk out looking fresher the same day, with no shedding phase to wait through.

The evidence supports the surface effect. In a 2024 imaging study that looked at skin microscopically before and after hydradermabrasion, researchers saw immediate thinning and smoothing of the stratum corneum and a more even, homogenised surface — the visual basis of "glow" — though notably they found no change in the quantity or quality of dermal collagen at two weeks. In other words, the science matches the honest promise: it refreshes the surface, it doesn't rebuild deep structure. A separate clinical study of a HydraFacial clarifying protocol in people with mild-to-moderate acne found that a series of six treatments improved overall skin appearance and congestion, with investigators and patients both rating skin as clearer. One session before an event gives you the surface refresh; a short series over weeks compounds it.

A very superficial peel (for example a light mandelic or lactic acid peel) can achieve something similar — brightening and smoothing — but it is more operator- and skin-dependent, and some peels cause light flaking a few days later. That's fine if it's timed so the flaking finishes before the event, and risky if it isn't. This is exactly the sort of judgement call that's worth a clinician rather than a last-minute guess, especially on deeper Indian skin tones where over-aggressive exfoliation can trigger post-inflammatory pigmentation — a dark mark that outlasts the event by months.

What to Book Earlier — Not the Day Before

Plenty of genuinely effective treatments make skin look worse for a few days before it looks better. Scheduling these too close to an event is the single most common pre-wedding skin regret we hear about. Give these proper lead time:

  • Medium-depth peels (e.g. higher-strength glycolic, salicylic or TCA): visible peeling and redness can last several days to a week. Book at least 2–4 weeks ahead. See our guide to chemical peels.
  • First-time retinoids or a stepped-up strength: these can trigger dryness, flaking and a temporary "purge" of breakouts for 2–6 weeks. Never start them in the final fortnight — the one time you want to debut a retinoid is not the month of your wedding.
  • Aggressive extractions or a strong clean-up facial: can leave transient redness and the odd post-extraction mark. Do these 1–2 weeks out so skin has time to settle.
  • Energy treatments (lasers, radiofrequency, microfocused ultrasound/HIFU): results build over weeks to months, and there can be short-term redness or swelling, so these belong in a longer plan, not in event week.
  • Pigmentation treatments (laser toning, peels for melasma): these need a course over weeks and can look patchy mid-way, so start early or not at all before a big day.

If your event is months away, that's actually good news — it means you can use the treatments that build real, lasting improvement rather than only surface polish. We've mapped that out in our 6-month pre-bridal skin timeline, and specifically for hair removal in our guide on starting laser hair removal before a wedding.

What's Mostly Hype: "Glow Drips" and IV Glutathione

Around every wedding and festive season, "glow drips" and IV glutathione get marketed as an injectable shortcut to bright, glowing skin. Here the evidence is clear enough to state plainly. Glutathione is an antioxidant with some anti-pigment (anti-melanogenic) activity in the lab, and there is modest evidence that oral and topical forms can give a mild, reversible lightening effect over weeks. But for the intravenous route specifically, the clinical evidence does not support efficacy, and reviews have repeatedly flagged safety concerns.

A 2024 systematic review of glutathione for skin lightening concluded that IV glutathione is contraindicated due to lack of efficacy and side effects, finding that oral and topical forms give only modest, unsustainable results. Earlier reviews in the Indian dermatology literature made the same point — that the popularity of IV glutathione is largely a marketing phenomenon, that the single supporting intravenous study had a flawed design, and that a national regulator had issued a public warning against its off-label use for skin lightening. "Brightening," even where it works at all, is also not the same thing as the surface glow you're actually after for an event — it changes pigment slowly, it doesn't smooth and hydrate the skin for Saturday.

The same caution applies to any first-time injectable, booster or "vitamin" drip right before a big day. Beyond the questionable benefit, you do not want to discover how your body reacts to something new 48 hours before you're on stage or on camera. The predictable, visible, same-week surface effect of a gentle facial is a far better use of the budget than an infusion the evidence doesn't back.

A Realistic Countdown Plan

Here's how we'd sequence the final stretch for most people — adjust the details with your clinician for your skin type and history:

  • 4+ weeks out: any peel series, retinoid ramp-up, pigmentation or active-acne treatment, and energy treatments if planned. This is where lasting change happens.
  • 1–2 weeks out: a clean-up/clarifying facial or a gentle superficial peel — but only if your skin tolerates it well and you've had it before. Start being strict with sunscreen and hydration now.
  • 2–3 days out: a HydraFacial or equivalent gentle, hydrating exfoliation — the main event-glow treatment. This is the sweet spot: enough time for any minor pinkness to settle, close enough that skin still looks freshly refreshed on the day.
  • Day before and morning of: nothing new or aggressive. Hydrate (a sheet mask you've used before is fine), moisturise, sunscreen by day, and sleep. Let the earlier work show through rather than piling on more.

Across the whole countdown, two unglamorous habits do more than any single treatment. The first is daily broad-spectrum sunscreen: a great deal of what reads as "dullness" and uneven tone in Indian skin is simply accumulated sun and tan, and nothing restores clarity like preventing more of it. The second is consistent hydration, inside and out — water through the day and a simple humectant-rich moisturiser do more for a dewy finish than most people expect.

Protecting the Glow on the Day

Once you've done the right treatment at the right time, a few small choices keep the result looking its best under event lighting and makeup. Keep the morning routine simple and familiar — cleanse, a hydrating serum you've used before, moisturiser, sunscreen — and avoid introducing a brand-new product you've never worn under makeup. Let skincare absorb for a few minutes before makeup so it doesn't pill. If your skin tends to look oily or shiny by evening (common in Mumbai humidity), a lightweight gel moisturiser and a mattifying primer only in the T-zone keeps the glow looking like healthy skin rather than slip. If it tends dry, a dewy finish and a hydrating mist help.

And give yourself a buffer: book the final facial so that even if your skin is mildly pink afterwards, there are 48–72 hours for it to settle completely. The goal of all of this is simple — on the day, your skin should look like your skin, rested and even and lit from within, not like something that was done to it that week.

The Bottom Line

You can absolutely look noticeably fresher and more radiant for an event — the realistic, evidence-backed route is a gentle exfoliating-and-hydrating treatment like a HydraFacial two to three days before, layered on top of weeks of good sunscreen and hydration. What doesn't work is leaving the strong stuff to the last minute, or paying for an IV "glow drip" the evidence doesn't support. The single rule that protects your skin on the day: never debut a new or aggressive treatment right before it matters. If you have more than a few weeks before your event, you have room to do far more than surface polish — and that's worth planning properly.
